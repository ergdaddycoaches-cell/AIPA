Materials and lighting

What to look for on the product sheet. Tile grip, flooring for walkers and wheels, lighting for older eyes, and hardware for stiff hands.

    The guides

    1. 1Start hereSlip resistance: reading a tile's DCOF ratingA tile's DCOF number tells you how well it grips when wet. Look for 0.42 or higher for any indoor floor that can get wet, and more for showers.
    2. 2Flooring that's kind to walkers and wheelsThe best floors for walkers and wheelchairs are firm, smooth and continuous from room to room. Thick carpet and high thresholds are the main problems.
    3. 3Why older eyes need more lightBy age 60, the eye lets in roughly a third of the light it did at 20. Older adults need far more light, spread evenly, to see clearly and safely.
    4. 4Glare, contrast and color temperatureGood lighting is not only about brightness. Controlling glare, adding contrast at edges and choosing the right color of light make a home easier to see.
    5. 5Lever handles, D-pulls and touch latchesHardware that works without gripping or twisting is easier for anyone with arthritis or weak hands. Swap knobs for levers and pulls first.

    Numbers worth knowing

    Questions to ask here

    Bring these to a contractor, or to the next Live Q&A.

    • What is this tile's DCOF rating when wet?
    • Will the flooring be smooth for a walker's wheels?
    • Is the finish matte or glossy?
    • What color temperature are the bulbs?
    • Can every handle be used with a closed fist?
    • Is there contrast at stair edges and counter edges?
