    The numbers worth knowing

    These measurements come from the 2010 ADA Standards and ANSI tile standards. The ADA doesn't apply to private homes, but its measurements are the best-tested guide there is.

    Read the full standards guide
    Key accessibility measurements and their sources

    Height of a grab bar33–36 inADA 2010, §609.4
    Clear opening of a doorway32 in min.ADA 2010, §404.2.3
    Toilet seat height17–19 inADA 2010, §604.4
    Knee space under a sink27 in highADA 2010, §306.3
    Space to turn a wheelchair60 in circleADA 2010, §304.3.1
    Steepest ramp slope1 : 12ADA 2010, §405.2

    The 10+ Year Freedom Plan

    Most homes don't need everything at once. Plan in stages, so each change is made before it's urgent.

    1

    Planning ahead

    Before anything has gone wrong.

    • Brighter, warmer lighting
    • Lever handles
    • Comfort-height toilet
    2

    Balance starts to change

    Small stumbles, more caution on stairs.

    • No thresholds to trip on
    • Grab bars that look like towel bars
    • Night lighting along paths
    3

    A cane or walker comes in

    Moving through the house takes planning.

    • Curbless shower
    • Wider doorways
    • Stairlift or main-floor bedroom
    4

    A caregiver helps daily

    Two people in the room at once.

    • Wheelchair turning space
    • Roll-under sink
    • Backing for a ceiling lift

    When you're ready for help

    1. Watch the webinar or call a guide

      Learn what's possible, what it costs and what to do first. No camera needed.

    2. Invite anyone who decides with you

      They can join the call. A guide will help you talk through the hard parts.

    3. Meet one vetted local specialist

      If you want an in-home visit, we introduce you to our partner contractor in your area.

    4. Judge the visit for yourself

      Use our checklist to see whether the inspection was thorough. You're never obligated to hire anyone.
